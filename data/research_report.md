# How are AI agents changing software engineering?

## Executive Summary

AI agents are significantly transforming software engineering by automating repetitive tasks, enhancing code generation and debugging, and redefining software quality and reliability. While they offer productivity gains and new capabilities, they also introduce risks and necessitate evolving skills and ethical considerations. The evidence highlights both opportunities and challenges, with gaps in understanding human-AI collaboration patterns and specific skill requirements. Long-term changes in practices and empirical methodologies are emerging, but further research is needed to fully assess their impact.

---

## 1. Automation of Software Development Tasks

AI agents are increasingly automating repetitive coding tasks such as debugging, refactoring, and code generation. This automation boosts productivity and reduces human error. For example, AI tools can streamline workflows by handling routine tasks, allowing developers to focus on more complex problems. The source emphasizes that these capabilities are critical for improving efficiency in software development processes. [Source](https://dev.to/raji_moshood_ee3a4c2638f6/how-to-automate-repetitive-coding-tasks-with-ai-322e)

---

## 2. Code Generation and Debugging

AI agents play a significant role in code generation and debugging by autonomously managing the software development workflow. Large language models (LLMs) enable agents to decompose tasks, generate code, and debug errors independently. Research indicates that these agents are part of a multi-agent LLM approach, where they collaborate to address complex software challenges. This autonomy enhances development speed but also raises questions about reliability and safety. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00083v1) [Source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388834987_Autonomous_Agents_in_Software_Engineering_A_Multi-Agent_LLM_Approach)

---

## 3. Human-AI Collaboration

**Available research evidence in this dataset is insufficient.** No reliable sources were found to address how human-AI collaboration patterns are evolving in software engineering workflows. Further studies are needed to explore this area.

---

## 4. Software Quality and Reliability

AI-infused test automation frameworks are enhancing software quality and reliability by integrating AI into testing processes. However, LLM-based agents also introduce new risks, including reliability, safety, and security concerns due to their autonomy and complex interactions. While AI can improve quality, its potential to create new vulnerabilities requires careful management. [Source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389362250_AI-Infused_Test_Automation_Frameworks_Enhancing_Software_Quality_and_Reliability) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17698)

---

## 5. Skills and Competencies

The evidence suggests that new skills are required for software engineers to integrate AI effectively, but specific competencies are not explicitly detailed. Sources mention categories such as technical core skills, LLM expertise, and soft skills, but they do not provide concrete examples of the required skills. This indicates a need for more granular guidance on skill development. [Source](https://aiengineerinsights.com/blog/ai-engineer-skills/) [Source](https://thealliance.ai/core-projects/guide-to-essential-competencies-for-ai)

---

## 6. Ethical Considerations

AI agents introduce new ethical risks, such as biases in training data and algorithmic decision-making. However, existing AI governance solutions, including tools for monitoring and addressing biases, can mitigate these risks. Developers and organizations are encouraged to adopt trustworthy AI standards to ensure ethical compliance. [Source](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/ai-agent-ethics)

---

## 7. Long-Term Changes in Software Engineering

AI agents are driving long-term changes in software engineering practices, including shifts in empirical methods and trust accountability frameworks. The empirical software engineering community must adapt its approaches to account for AI as an active collaborator rather than a passive tool. These changes highlight the need for updated validation standards and research methodologies. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10819v1) [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08108v2)

---

## Overall Assessment

The strongest findings indicate that AI agents are automating repetitive tasks, enhancing code generation and debugging, and improving software quality through test automation. However, their autonomy introduces risks that require governance and ethical safeguards. While the evidence supports the transformative potential of AI in software engineering, gaps remain in understanding human-AI collaboration and specific skill requirements. Long-term changes in practices and methodologies are emerging, but further research is needed to fully assess their implications.

Well-supported conclusions include the automation of coding tasks and the role of AI in code generation. Weaker evidence exists for specific skill requirements and the exact nature of human-AI collaboration patterns. Conflicting evidence arises in the software quality section, where AI is both a tool for improvement and a source of new risks.

---

## Limitations

Key limitations include:  
- **Insufficient evidence** for human-AI collaboration patterns.  
- **Low-confidence claims** about specific skills required for AI integration.  
- **Varied source quality**, with some studies lacking detailed empirical data.  
- **Limited source coverage**, particularly in areas like ethical frameworks and long-term practice shifts.  
- **Conflicting evidence** on software quality, where AI both enhances and risks reliability.
